# Reconcile durable knowledge

Locate the affected note, its original sources, and inbound references. Establish the subject and scope of each claim: a proposal, decision, observed runtime state, and user preference can all differ without contradiction.

Identify the authoritative evidence for an update. A newer timestamp alone is not sufficient. For changing facts, obtain current evidence when available; otherwise label the claim unverified and keep the conflict explicit. Do not turn a one-off instruction into a permanent preference without user intent to retain it.

When the user requests reconciliation, update the smallest relevant note. Preserve the prior decision and its date in a superseded/history section when it explains the change. Add the new evidence, effective date if known, and links to the decision that replaces it. Keep raw source content intact. Avoid creating multiple competing canonical notes.

If evidence cannot resolve a conflict, record both positions with their sources and the precise open question. Do not choose arbitrarily or overwrite a higher-authority decision. Ask only for the missing decision that prevents the requested update.

Before moving or merging a note, inspect inbound references and preserve a redirect or repair the references within the authorized scope. Check the final diff, source links, and index entry. Report what changed, what remains historical, and which claims still need verification. Reading a note or answering a question does not by itself authorize writing durable memory.
